A little courage

The Lantern Garden

High above the clouds, the garden was turning purple and gold. Little lanterns glowed beneath a tree full of blossoms.

Blue Toby stopped before the bridge. “It looks different now,” he said. “Will you walk beside me?”

“Of course,” said Taboshi. “Look! We can still see the flowers by the path.”

“We can take our time,” said Patience. The three friends crossed the little bridge together.

On the other side, Toby looked up at a lantern. “I’m glad I asked,” he said. There was room for all three beneath its warm light.

Look and talk together

Find a lantern, a bridge, and a pink flower in the picture. When have you asked someone to stay beside you?

A little kindness

One Kind Step

The leafy path curled through the floating forest. Blue Toby stepped onto a smooth round stone. Then another. There was so much to see!

“Toby, wait a moment!” called Taboshi. Beside the path, a tiny sprout had opened two green leaves.

Toby looked back. His friends had stopped to admire it. He could hurry ahead—or come and see what they had found.

He stepped back beside them. “Hello, little leaves,” he whispered.

When they were ready, the friends set off together. This time, Toby looked back after each little step. The path felt even better with everyone along.

Look and talk together

Can you find the tiny sprout? Hold up two fingers for its two leaves. What could you do when a friend needs a moment?

A little discovery

The Pond of Three Little Stumps

“One, two, three!” said Blue Toby. Three little wooden stumps stood in the blue pond. The friends sat on the grassy bank to look.

“I see pink flowers,” said Taboshi.

“I see little circles in the water,” said Patience. A gentle ripple spread around a stump.

Toby leaned closer to his friends. “I see a fish!” They watched a bright little shape slip past a lily pad.

It was the same pond, but each friend had noticed something different. “Let’s tell each other one more thing,” said Toby. And their little afternoon filled with discoveries.

Look and talk together

Point to each wooden stump and count to three. Take turns naming something you see. Did your reading friend notice something different?
